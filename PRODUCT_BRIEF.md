# Product Brief

## Working name

AI Vendor Claim Auditor

The name is descriptive but provisional. Avoid naming that implies certification, legal review, or a definitive truth judgment.

## Problem

People evaluating AI-enabled software must reconcile claims spread across marketing pages, trust centers, security documents, data-processing agreements, model cards, technical papers, and sales responses.

The immediate pain is evidence triage:

- Which claims materially affect our intended use?
- What evidence supports each claim?
- Where is the evidence incomplete, irrelevant, stale, or contradictory?
- What should we ask the vendor before approval?

Existing vendor-risk and AI-governance platforms can address broad enterprise programs, but they are heavier than a small team needs for a single pre-purchase review.

## Target user

Initial user:

- An AI governance, security, privacy, procurement, or operations lead
- At a small or midsize organization
- Evaluating an AI-enabled SaaS product
- Without a mature AI-governance platform or dedicated evaluation team

The first round of customer discovery must identify one primary role. Do not market to all five roles simultaneously.

## Job to be done

When I am evaluating an AI-enabled vendor, help me find material claim–evidence gaps and generate precise follow-up questions so I can move the review forward without reading every document manually.

## Value proposition

Upload a vendor's public claims and supporting materials. Receive a cited evidence-gap brief and the five questions to ask before approval.

## Primary output

A decision brief containing:

- Intended-use context
- Material claims with exact source citations
- Evidence associated with each claim
- Evidence status
- Why the gap matters for this use
- Prioritized vendor follow-up questions
- Clear limitations and review scope

## Evidence statuses

Use only:

- **Supported by supplied evidence**
- **Partially supported**
- **No evidence found in supplied materials**
- **Conflicting disclosure**
- **Not assessable from supplied materials**

These describe the evidence packet, not objective truth.

## Differentiation

The initial wedge is deliberately narrower than enterprise governance platforms:

- One review packet instead of an AI inventory
- A decision brief instead of a governance dashboard
- Evidence gaps instead of a universal vendor score
- Buyer-ready questions instead of a long generic questionnaire
- Minimal setup and no integrations

## Risks

- Users may mistake evidence status for a legal or safety conclusion.
- Private vendor documents may create security and retention concerns.
- Model-generated citations or claim mappings may be inaccurate.
- The product may save reading time without improving the decision.
- Buyers may already solve this adequately with general-purpose AI tools.

These risks should be tested before the product expands.
