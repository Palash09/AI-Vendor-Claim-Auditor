# Validation Plan

## Decision to validate

Will buyers use and pay for a cited claim–evidence gap brief before approving an AI-enabled vendor?

## Riskiest assumptions

1. Buyers experience enough repeated document-review pain to change their workflow.
2. The five selected claim domains cover a meaningful portion of the review.
3. A cited evidence-gap brief improves the decision, not only the reading speed.
4. Buyers are willing and permitted to upload vendor documents.
5. The output is differentiated from using a general-purpose AI assistant with a prompt.

## Concierge pilot

Recruit 8–10 participants who evaluated an AI-enabled vendor within the previous three months.

Ask each participant for:

- One real vendor evidence packet
- The intended use
- Their recent review process and approximate time spent
- Questions they ultimately asked the vendor

Produce the proposed audit manually behind a simple intake form. Do not build workflow integrations or a polished application first.

## Interview prompts

- Walk me through your most recent AI-vendor review.
- Which documents did you inspect, and in what order?
- Where did you wait on the vendor or another internal reviewer?
- Which claim was hardest to verify?
- What question did you almost miss?
- How do you document the final decision today?
- Which documents could you upload to a new tool?
- What would make this output unsafe or unusable?

Avoid asking whether the participant “likes the idea.” Focus on recent behavior and real artifacts.

## Go/no-go targets

Advance to a coded MVP only if:

- At least 60% of participants identify one generated question they would actually send.
- Median review time falls at least 30% against each participant's recent baseline.
- At least three participants agree to pay approximately $50 per audit or $200 per month for repeated use.
- No critical citation error remains after human review in the final three pilots.

These are decision thresholds, not forecasts.

## Primary metric

**Questions acted on per audit**

Count a question only when the participant sends it to the vendor, adds it to an assessment, or uses it in an approval decision.

## Secondary metrics

- Time from upload to reviewed brief
- Claims retained after human review
- Findings removed as incorrect or irrelevant
- Follow-up questions edited before use
- Participants returning with a second vendor
- Willingness to upload private documents

## Failure signals

- Users value the summary but do not act on any question.
- Most useful findings require independent testing rather than document review.
- Citation correction takes as long as manual review.
- Users cannot upload the documents that matter.
- A generic AI prompt produces an equivalent trusted result.
- The buyer is unclear or differs materially across every pilot.

## Next decision after the pilot

Choose one:

- Stop: the problem is weak or adequately solved.
- Narrow further: focus on one claim domain or buyer role.
- Build the MVP: automate the validated concierge workflow.
- Change delivery: offer a service or review template instead of software.
